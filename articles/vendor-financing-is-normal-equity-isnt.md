# Vendor Financing Is Normal. Equity Isn't.

The most normal-sounding sentence in AI finance right now is also the least normal thing about it: *"it's just vendor deals, happens in every industry."*

It does happen in every industry. Companies finance their suppliers' equipment all the time. Dell Financial Services leases servers. Cisco Capital finances routers. A chipmaker extending credit to a customer is as boring as a car loan. The defenders of the AI buildout are right about that part.

What they don't mention is the equity.

In every industry, vendor financing gets paid in cash, secured by the hardware. In AI, the vendors are getting paid in cash *and* ownership of the customer — and then booking the customer's purchases as revenue. Nvidia has now spent **$40 billion on equity bets in its own customers**, including **$30 billion into OpenAI** ([CNBC, May 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/09/nvidia-embraces-ai-investor-topping-40-billion-in-equity-bets-2026.html)). Microsoft owns a reported ~49% of OpenAI's economics *and* books OpenAI's Azure spend as its own revenue — analysts at [wheresyoured.at](https://www.wheresyoured.at/oai_docs/) estimate that roughly **70% of Microsoft's reported AI revenue — more than 7% of its entire fiscal-year revenue — traces back to one customer: OpenAI**, spending money that substantially originated from Microsoft itself. Amazon has invested ~$8 billion in Anthropic — a stake now worth **~$74 billion on paper** ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/amazon-google-billions-anthropic-ipo/)) — while Anthropic commits to spend [$100 billion on AWS compute](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/24/google-amazon-anthropic-investment). Google holds $3 billion of Anthropic equity and a cloud deal worth tens of billions.

That's not vendor financing. That's a circle. And the circle is now most of the money.

**The Highlights:**
- Vendor financing, in every normal industry, is cash for equipment, secured by the hardware — **no equity, ever**
- AI vendor deals come with stock: Nvidia $40B in equity bets (incl. $30B OpenAI, 6-7% of CoreWeave), Microsoft ~49% of OpenAI, Amazon ~$8B→$74B paper in Anthropic, Google $3B
- The same dollars count twice: Microsoft's AI revenue is ~70% OpenAI — money that began as Microsoft's own investment
- OpenAI's named compute commitments alone: Azure $250B, Oracle $300B, AWS $138B, CoreWeave $22.4B, Nvidia up to $100B, Broadcom $10B — **$820B+ of commitments, 2025–2035**
- The collateral is the same GPUs, the depreciation is flattered (~$176B by one estimate), and the shortfall the loop must close is ~$750B in 2026 alone
- A loop is stable while outside money keeps flowing in. The outside money is debt. Watch the debt market — that's where the circle meets reality

## What Normal Vendor Financing Looks Like

Dell Financial Services: leases servers to customers, gets paid monthly, takes the hardware back if the customer stops paying. Cisco Capital: finances network equipment, same structure. GE Capital, IBM Global Financing, every equipment-financing arm of every industrial company — the pattern is ancient and boring: **the vendor finances the thing it sells, the loan is secured by the thing itself, and the vendor has zero ownership of the customer.**

The economics are clean. The vendor's revenue is real because it's paid in cash from outside the relationship. The customer's risk is bounded because the collateral is the equipment, not the customer's survival. If the customer goes bankrupt, the vendor repossesses the gear and sells it to someone else. Nobody's equity depends on anybody else's continued buying.

This is why "it's just vendor financing" sounds so reassuring. It describes a structure where the money moves one way and gets repaid from real operations.

## What AI Does Instead

Now look at the actual structure of the AI buildout. [Bloomberg's mapping of the circular deals](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-ai-circular-deals/) is the definitive chart of it: the vendors are also the investors, the customers are also the investors' equity, and the revenue is the loop itself.

**The Nvidia loop.** Nvidia invests in the labs and neoclouds that buy its chips. The labs use Nvidia's money to buy Nvidia's chips. Nvidia books the sale as revenue — and books the equity appreciation on the lab as a gain on its investment portfolio. The lab's valuation rises in part *because* it's buying Nvidia's chips at scale. Nvidia's single largest equity bet is $30 billion in OpenAI ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/09/nvidia-embraces-ai-investor-topping-40-billion-in-equity-bets-2026.html)); it holds 6.3% of CoreWeave ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/nvidia-invests-2-billion-coreweave-expands-partnership-2026-01-26/)) while CoreWeave signed [$22.4 billion of compute agreements with OpenAI](https://blogs.cuit.columbia.edu/gjb2124/circular-financing/) — the vendor's equity returns tied directly to the customer's compute consumption. And it's now reportedly [guaranteeing OpenAI's data center debt](https://financialpost.com/technology/nvidia-750-billion-deals-revive-fear-ai-circular-financing) — the latest escalation of what the Financial Post calls "vendor financing that's already under scrutiny."

**The Microsoft loop.** Microsoft invested ~$13B in OpenAI and owns a large share of its economics (now capped, [running through 2030](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/27/openai-microsoft-partnership-revenue-cap.html)). OpenAI's compute runs on Azure — [45% of Microsoft's remaining performance obligations trace to OpenAI](https://www.constellationr.com/insights/news/microsoft-q2-strong-azure-growth-39-openai-45-rpo) — and Microsoft also pays OpenAI 20% of the revenue from reselling OpenAI's models. The analysts at [wheresyoured.at](https://www.wheresyoured.at/oai_docs/) put the resulting circularity in one sentence: Microsoft's AI revenue is substantially OpenAI spending money that substantially originated from Microsoft.

**The Anthropic loop.** Amazon invested ~$8B in Anthropic across installments since 2023 — worth ~$74B on paper as of April ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/amazon-google-billions-anthropic-ipo/)) — and agreed in April 2026 to invest [up to $25 billion more](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/04/20/technology/amazon-anthropic-investment.html). In exchange, Anthropic committed to spend [$100 billion for up to 5 gigawatts of AWS compute](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/24/google-amazon-anthropic-investment). Google holds $3 billion of Anthropic equity plus a cloud deal worth tens of billions ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2025/10/23/anthropic-google-cloud-deal-tpu.html)). Anthropic's IPO — when it comes — will finally put a market number on all of it.

**The OpenAI commitment stack.** The sum of OpenAI's named compute commitments, as tracked by [the AI circular economy analysis](https://ai-circular-economy.com/):

| Committed to | Amount (2025–2035) |
|---|---|
| Oracle | $300B |
| Azure (Microsoft) | $250B |
| AWS | $138B |
| Nvidia | up to $100B |
| CoreWeave | $22.4B |
| Broadcom | $10B |
| **Total named** | **$820B+** |

That's three-quarters of a trillion dollars of commitments — to companies that, in every case except one, also own a piece of OpenAI. Dell has never needed to own a piece of its customers to sell them servers. It just needed them to pay.

## Why the Equity Changes Everything

If the vendors were just lending money against hardware, the structure would be ugly but normal. The equity is what turns it into a loop, and it does three specific things:

**1. The same dollar counts twice.** Microsoft books OpenAI's Azure spending as revenue — and the value of its OpenAI stake rises as OpenAI spends more. Nvidia books the chip sale as revenue and the lab's rising valuation as portfolio gain. One dollar of outside capital becomes two dollars of reported financial health. This isn't fraud — it's disclosed, it's real money moving — but it means the *system's* reported revenue is partly self-referential. [The wheresyoured.at analysis](https://explainx.ai/blog/microsoft-openai-circular-ai-revenue-gpu-depreciation-2026) of Microsoft's filings: ~70% of AI revenue, >7% of total revenue, from one customer whose money "substantially originated" from the investor side of the same relationship.

**2. Survival is mutual — which means risk is correlated.** In a normal vendor deal, if the customer dies, the vendor repossesses and moves on. In a circular deal, if the customer dies, the vendor loses the revenue *and* the equity — simultaneously. Every participant in the loop has the same counterparty risk, which is exactly what a diversified system is supposed to avoid. The [Bain/industry analysis](https://ai-circular-economy.com/) puts the gap the loop must close by 2030 at hundreds of billions; [CreditSights](https://ai-circular-economy.com/) estimates the top-five hyperscalers alone face a ~$750B shortfall in 2026. The equity doesn't close the gap — it just makes everyone feel it at the same time.

**3. The collateral is the same GPUs, depreciating on paper slower than in reality.** The [CFA Institute's capex analysis](https://alcapitaladvisory.com/research/intelligence/ai-infrastructure.html) flags the accounting gap Michael Burry and others have hammered on: hyperscalers depreciate AI hardware over 5–6 years while its economic life is closer to 2–3, flattering reported earnings by roughly **$176 billion across 2026–2028**. In a circular structure this matters twice: the revenue inside the loop is partly paper (equity appreciation), and the hardware underpinning it is partly paper (understated depreciation). The collateral is the same GPUs — and the GPUs are aging faster than the books say.

## Loop, Not Bubble — and the Debt Market Is the Outside

A bubble pops when assets are overpriced and the bid disappears. A loop is different: it doesn't pop, it *starves*. It's stable as long as money keeps flowing in from outside the circle. The question for AI financing isn't "is this a bubble?" — it's "who is outside the loop, and are they still willing to fund it?"

The answer, increasingly, is the debt market. US tech companies borrowed **$300 billion in the first seven months of 2026**, with JP Morgan expecting $200 billion more by year-end — the financing that underwrites the buildout my earlier post on the [iPhone-victim arithmetic](/post/ai-bubble-iphone-victim) walked through. Nvidia is now [guaranteeing data center debt](https://financialpost.com/technology/nvidia-750-billion-deals-revive-fear-ai-circular-financing) — which moves the guarantee *inside* the loop, defeating the point of debt as an outside check. When a vendor guarantees the customer's debt, the "outside" money has been wrapped into the circle too.

That's the moment the defenders' story breaks. Vendor financing is normal because it's *boring* — cash, collateral, no ownership, no shared fate. The AI version is none of those things: it's equity, paper revenue, mutual survival, and now mutual debt. Calling it "just vendor deals" is technically true the way "just a car loan" is true of a loan where the lender also owns the car company and books the dealership's sales as revenue.

## What to Watch

- **Nvidia's guarantees** — how much OpenAI data-center debt Nvidia is on the hook for, and whether it shows up on anyone's balance sheet
- **Anthropic's IPO** — the first time the loop's paper values meet a real market bid for a major lab
- **The depreciation debate** — if regulators or auditors force shorter hardware lives, ~$176B of flattered earnings starts leaking out of the loop
- **Debt-market appetite** — the next rate hiccup or credit event is the real test; the loop's outside funding is the only part of it that can actually say no
- **CoreWeave's churn** — a neocloud whose two biggest "customers" are also its investors and supplier

The closing line, from my earlier post on [why the price war was the warning](/post/the-power-war): letters don't ship, models do. The same logic applies here. A normal vendor is paid in cash. These vendors are paid in ownership of the thing they sell to — and the revenue they report is the same money going around in a circle. That's not financing. That's a circle. And circles are beautiful until the outside stops feeding them.
